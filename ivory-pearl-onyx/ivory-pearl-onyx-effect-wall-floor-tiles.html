What is an Ivory Pearl onyx effect tile?

An Ivory Pearl onyx effect tile is a glazed porcelain tile printed to look like ivory onyx stone. The tile body is pressed clay fired at high heat, so it is dense, hard and close to waterproof. The 60×120cm polished version has a stated water absorption below 0.5%. That figure places it in the BIa group, the most water-resistant class of pressed tile in the EN 14411 (ISO 13006) tile standard.

The design copies the look of real onyx: a soft ivory ground, veins that move in long drifts, and pockets of pearl and pale honey. It is not stone. Most decorative "onyx" sold for walls and floors is banded calcite. That stone sits at about 3 on the Mohs hardness scale, scratches easily, and etches when acids such as lemon juice touch it. Real onyx also needs sealing. Ivory Pearl porcelain needs none of that care.

There is one honest trade-off. Real onyx is translucent, so it can be lit from behind. Porcelain is opaque, so Ivory Pearl cannot be backlit. Its "glow" comes from the printed depth and, on the polished and gloss versions, from reflected light.

Is Ivory Pearl ivory, cream, beige or pearl white?

Ivory Pearl reads as warm ivory: paler than beige, creamier than white. In practice, the colour words line up like this:

  • Pearl white is cool and close to white. Our Ice Onyx Pearl White sits here.
  • Ivory is white with a little yellow warmth. Ivory Pearl sits here.
  • Cream has more yellow and a touch of beige. Onyx Cream sits here.
  • Beige adds grey-brown. Alba Onyx Beige sits here.

Light changes how ivory looks. Warm white bulbs (around 2700K to 3000K) keep it creamy and soft. Cool daylight bulbs (4000K and above) push it whiter and slightly greyer. Order a sample and look at it under your own bulbs at night, as well as in daylight.

What does the R9 slip rating mean for the polished tile?

R9 is the lowest band of the German DIN 51130 ramp test, which suits dry indoor floors. In that test, a person in safety boots walks on an oiled tile while the ramp tilts. R9 means the tile stayed safe between 6° and 10°. R10 covers 10° to 19°, and R11 covers 19° to 27°. UK slip advice often uses the pendulum test instead, where a wet reading of 36 or more means low slip potential. Ivory Pearl's gloss and matt versions do not publish either figure yet. So use the matt for grip, and use a tile with a published R11 rating on a level-access shower floor.

Should you buy Ivory Pearl in 60×120cm or 60×60cm?

Buy 60×120cm for long walls, open-plan floors and any space where you want fewer grout lines. Buy 60×60cm for small bathrooms, en-suites and cloakrooms, where a 1.2m tile would need too many cuts. The size also decides the finish, because the 60×120 comes only polished and the 60×60 comes in gloss or matt.

  • Tiles per m²: one 60×120 tile covers 0.72m², so you need about 1.39 tiles per m². One 60×60 tile covers 0.36m², so you need about 2.78 per m².
  • Grout lines: a 2.4m × 2.4m wall in 60×120 tiles laid landscape has 9.6m of grout joint. The same wall in 60×60 tiles has 14.4m, half as much again. Fewer joints make the wall read as one slab.
  • Layouts: the 60×60 can be laid straight, brick-bond or diagonal. The 60×120 looks best straight or with an offset of no more than one third of its length.
  • Floor flatness: a 1.2m tile shows any dip in the floor. A 60×60 is more forgiving on older floors.
  • Handling: a 60×120 tile is awkward for one person to carry and lay. The 60×60 is much easier to lift and place.

The 60×120 polished run includes four different faces. Open several boxes before you start and mix the tiles, so no two matching faces sit next to each other. Both sizes have rectified edges, which are ground perfectly straight so the joints can stay tight at 2mm to 3mm.

How do you fit 60×120 polished Ivory Pearl without lippage?

You fit 60×120 polished Ivory Pearl on a flat base, with full adhesive cover and a levelling system. Lippage is when one tile edge sits higher than the next. On a polished face it catches the light and shows at once. These steps prevent it:

  1. Check the base. Lay a 2m straightedge in several directions. A common target is no gap bigger than 3mm. Self-level the floor first if it fails.
  2. Use the right adhesive. Pick a flexible, S1-rated tile adhesive for porcelain. Over underfloor heating, keep the heating off until the adhesive and grout have cured, following the adhesive maker's times.
  3. Back-butter every tile. Spread a thin coat on the tile back as well as the floor or wall. Aim for at least 95% adhesive contact, with no hollow spots under the corners.
  4. Use levelling clips. They hold neighbouring edges flush while the adhesive sets.
  5. Keep the offset small. Stagger 60×120 tiles by no more than one third of their length. A half-bond offset makes any slight bow in a long tile show as a raised edge.
  6. Keep joints at 2mm to 3mm. Rectified edges allow it, and narrow joints suit the onyx look.
  7. Protect the face. Keep grit off the polished surface during the job. Clean adhesive off the face before it dries.
  8. Check before you fix. Open several boxes, check shade and faces in daylight, and report any problem before tiles are cut. Cut or fixed tiles cannot be returned.

What goes with Ivory Pearl: metals, wood, paint and worktops?

Warm materials suit Ivory Pearl best, because they share its honey undertone. Cold, blue-white materials make it look more yellow by contrast.

  • Metals: brushed brass and warm gold suit it best. Matt black works if you want contrast. Polished chrome is fine but reads cooler.
  • Wood: oak, walnut and other mid-tone timbers for vanities, shelves and doors.
  • Paint: warm whites, soft greige and muted sage on untiled walls. Avoid stark brilliant white next to the tile.
  • Worktops and sanitaryware: warm white or cream quartz, and white or ivory ceramic basins and baths.
  • Textiles: linen, oatmeal and caramel towels and blinds pick up the honey veins.

How do you clean and look after Ivory Pearl tiles?

Clean Ivory Pearl with warm water, a pH-neutral cleaner and a soft microfibre mop or cloth. The tiles never need sealing. A few habits keep the polished and gloss faces streak-free:

  • Use very little cleaner. Too much leaves a film that shows as streaks on a shiny tile.
  • Buff polished and gloss tiles dry with a clean microfibre cloth after mopping.
  • Avoid abrasive creams and scouring pads. Over time they dull a polished face.
  • Put felt pads under chair and table legs, and a doormat at entrances. Grit is the main cause of fine scratches.
  • Wipe shower walls with a squeegee after use to stop limescale spots on the gloss.
  • Clean grout lines now and then. A grout sealer is optional, but it helps light grout resist stains.

Ivory Pearl onyx effect tiles: questions buyers ask

Is Ivory Pearl real onyx stone?

No, Ivory Pearl is porcelain printed with an onyx design. It looks like ivory onyx but is harder, non-porous and needs no sealing.

Are Ivory Pearl tiles slippery?

Yes, the polished and gloss versions are slippery when wet, like any shiny tile. The 60×120 polished is rated R9, which suits dry floors. Use the 60×60 matt on bathroom and kitchen floors.

Can I use Ivory Pearl in a shower?

Yes, all three versions can go on shower walls. Do not use the polished or gloss on the shower floor. Use a shower tray, or a floor tile with a published R11 rating for a level-access shower.

Can Ivory Pearl go on a kitchen floor?

Yes, use the 60×60 matt on a kitchen floor. Spills and grease make polished and gloss floors slippery, so keep those for the splashback.

Do Ivory Pearl tiles need sealing?

No, Ivory Pearl is glazed porcelain and does not need sealing. You can seal the grout if you want extra stain resistance.

Will the polished surface scratch?

Porcelain resists scratches far better than real onyx, but grit dragged across a polished face can leave fine marks. Felt pads, doormats and regular sweeping prevent most of them.

Can I lay Ivory Pearl over underfloor heating?

Yes, all three versions suit underfloor heating. Use a flexible adhesive and follow the curing times before you switch the heating on. Our guide to porcelain floor tiles over living room underfloor heating explains the set-up.

Can I use Ivory Pearl outside?

No, Ivory Pearl is an indoor tile. A polished or gloss face is unsafe outdoors in UK rain and frost.

Do the 60×120 polished and 60×60 gloss match exactly?

They share the same print and colour, but the surfaces differ. The 60×120 is mechanically polished and the 60×60 is a glazed gloss. On separate surfaces, such as walls and floor, they read as one range.

Does the matt look different from the polished?

Slightly, yes. Side by side, the matt looks a little softer and paler because it does not reflect light. On a floor below polished walls, the difference reads as a natural change of finish.

How many Ivory Pearl tiles are in a square metre?

About 1.39 tiles of 60×120cm, or about 2.78 tiles of 60×60cm. Every box covers 1.44m²: two 60×120 tiles or four 60×60 tiles.

Can Ivory Pearl be backlit like real onyx?

No, porcelain is opaque, so light cannot pass through it. For a glowing effect, light the wall from above or below with LED strips instead.

Does ivory tile look yellow?

It can look more yellow under cool daylight bulbs or beside brilliant white paint and grout. Warm white bulbs and warm white grout keep it looking creamy.

Is ivory onyx a good choice for a small bathroom?

Yes, a pale, light-reflecting tile helps a small bathroom feel bigger. Use the 60×60 gloss on the walls and the 60×60 matt on the floor, since a 60×120 tile needs many cuts in a tight room. For more ideas, see our article on onyx tiles in luxury bathrooms.
