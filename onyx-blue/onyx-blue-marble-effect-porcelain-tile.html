What Colour Is Onyx Blue, Really?

Onyx Blue is a mid-tone blue-grey, close to slate or stormy sky. It is not navy, and it is not turquoise. White veins run across every tile like cracks in ice, and some faces carry rust-amber flecks along the darker seams. Those warm flecks are why the tile pairs so well with brass and wood, as covered further down.

Light changes how it looks. Cool daylight from a north-facing window pushes it greyer. Warm bulbs bring out more of the amber. Screens change it again, which is why we suggest ordering a sample before a full floor. The product page offers a £1.99 cut sample or a £7.99 20×50cm sample.

Is Onyx Blue real onyx or real marble?

Neither. It is porcelain, and "onyx" and "marble effect" describe the look, not the material. Natural onyx and marble are two different stones, and stone sold as "blue onyx" is scarce. Much of the blue onyx used in jewellery is dyed. A porcelain tile avoids that problem, because the colour is fired into the glaze. Porcelain is also far denser than either stone, so it does not etch when lemon juice or cleaning sprays touch it. Our comparison of marble-effect porcelain and real marble explains the trade-offs in more depth.

Why does every tile look different?

The onyx design is printed from a set of different faces, so neighbouring tiles rarely repeat. That is what makes a floor look like stone rather than wallpaper. To spread the pattern evenly, open three or four boxes at once and lay the tiles out dry before fixing. Move any two dramatic, dark-seamed faces apart, and put calmer faces where furniture will sit.

What Goes With Onyx Blue?

The amber in the veins is the key to styling this tile. Warm metals and natural wood pick it up and warm the room. An all-grey, all-chrome scheme can feel cold against a blue-grey floor.

Brass, bronze and gold: brushed brass taps, bronze mirror frames and gold towel rails all suit Onyx Blue, as the photo above shows. If you want more gold, the Persian Onyx Gold Blue 60×60cm works as a contrast panel or a floor in a small en-suite.

Wood and terracotta: oak vanities, walnut shelving and burnt-orange textiles balance the cool base. The bathroom shown earlier uses taupe sanitaryware and an amber robe for the same reason.

Charcoal and black: dark sofas, matt black frames and black taps give a moody, hotel-style finish. Keep the walls light if the room is small.

Walls around a blue floor: warm white, soft putty and pale grey let the floor lead. Strong blue paint next to the tile tends to clash, because two different blues rarely sit happily side by side.

Grout: a mid grey blends into the base colour. Mapei Ultracolor Plus 112 Medium Grey is the safe choice for walls and floors. Mapei 114 Anthracite gives a slightly darker line that matches the deepest seams and hides wear on busy floors. Avoid bright white grout, because it outlines every tile and turns a stone-look floor into a grid. Other shades are in our grout collection.

How Do You Fit and Look After Onyx Blue?

Fitting: a 1.2m polished tile shows every dip in the floor, because light catches a raised edge. Start with a flat, sound base and use a levelling clip system. Spread adhesive on the floor and on the back of each tile for a full bed with no hollow spots. The rectified edges allow a 2mm joint. Then follow our step-by-step grouting guide, wiping haze off the gloss before it dries.

Sealing: our listing says the porcelain face does not need sealing. Polishing can leave microscopic pores in the glaze, though, so do a quick test first. Rub a little of your chosen grout onto a spare tile, wait ten minutes and wipe it off. If it leaves a shadow, apply a porcelain sealer before grouting. That small step prevents the most common complaint about polished tiles.

Cleaning: sweep or vacuum grit first, because grit is what dulls a polished surface. Then mop with warm water and a pH-neutral cleaner, and buff dry with microfibre for a streak-free shine. Skip vinegar, bleach-heavy sprays and scouring pads. A mid-tone blue-grey hides dust better than white or black tiles, which is one reason it suits busy family rooms. Is there a matt version of Onyx Blue?

Not under the same name. The closest matt option is the Sylvan Blue Marble Essence matt anti-slip 60×120cm, which has a blue marble look in a grippier finish.

Can I use Onyx Blue in a shower?

On the walls, yes. On the shower floor, no. Use a shower tray or a matt anti-slip tile there instead.

Is Onyx Blue too dark for a small bathroom?

Not if you balance it. The polished face reflects light, and the colour is a mid-tone rather than a deep navy.
