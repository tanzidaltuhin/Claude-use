Cream, Beige or Ivory: Where Does Onyx Cream Sit?

Onyx Cream sits between ivory and beige. Ivory is paler and closer to white. Beige is darker and leans towards sand or taupe. Onyx Cream has the warmth of beige with the lightness of ivory, rather like the top of a latte. Its veins are soft and cloudy, not sharp black lines, so the tile reads as calm from across a room and only shows its pattern close up.

That calmness is why cream works across a whole ground floor without tiring the eye. If you want a paler, cooler stone look, compare it with our cream onyx tiles collection, which puts the ivory and honey shades side by side. For more on choosing between beige marble looks by finish and slip rating, read our beige marble tiles guide.

Is Onyx Cream real onyx?

No. Onyx Cream is porcelain, a dense fired clay, with an onyx pattern printed into the glaze. Real cream and honey onyx is a soft, translucent stone that marks easily and needs regular sealing. The porcelain version keeps the look without the upkeep. The product listing confirms that the face does not need sealing. Our comparison of marble-effect porcelain and real marble explains the trade-offs in more depth.

Will cream tiles look dated in a few years?

Cream tiles get a dated reputation from small glossy squares with thick dark grout, a look that was common decades ago. A large rectified tile with soft veining and a matching grout looks nothing like that. Fewer joints and a stone pattern read as current, especially next to oak, black metal or brushed brass. The colour of the grout matters more to the "dated" question than the colour of the tile.

Where Should You Use Onyx Cream?

Living rooms, hallways and kitchen-diners

This is where Onyx Cream does its best work. A dry floor lets the polish shine safely, and the warm tone suits oak, walnut and grey sofas alike. Put a barrier mat inside the front door, because grit and rain are what dull and wet a polished hallway floor. Over heating it is a comfortable floor in winter. What wall colours go with cream tiles?

Warm white, soft greige and mushroom tones let the tile lead. Sage and olive greens bring out the warmth of the cream, and dusty blue gives a cooler contrast. Avoid a stark brilliant white next to cream tiles, because it makes the cream look yellow. With grey walls, choose a warm grey rather than a blue-grey, so the two do not clash.

Cream is easy to pair. Brushed brass and gold warm it up for a classic hotel feel. Matt black taps and frames sharpen it, as in the shower room above. Fitting and Living With a Polished Cream Floor

Before tiling: a polished face shows every lip between tiles, so the floor must be flat. Use levelling clips with the 60×120cm size, and back-butter each tile so there are no hollow spots under the tile. The rectified edges allow a 2mm joint. Fix with a flexible S1 cement-based adhesive. Over underfloor heating, bring the system up to temperature slowly the first time.

Grouting: work in small areas and wipe haze off the polish before it dries. Our guide to grouting tiles walks through each step.

Sealing: our listing says Onyx Cream does not need sealing. Some retailers recommend a sealer for polished porcelain, because polishing can open tiny pores. To settle it for your tiles, smear a little of your grout on a spare tile and wipe it off after ten minutes. A clean wipe means no sealer is needed. A faint stain means you should apply a porcelain sealer before grouting.

Everyday cleaning: sweep or vacuum first, then mop with warm water and a pH-neutral cleaner. Buff dry with microfibre for a streak-free shine. Cream hides light dust well, but it shows dark scuffs.

Grey marks: the grey streaks you sometimes see on light porcelain are usually metal rubbed off a shoe heel, a chair leg or a pan base. They sit on the surface, not in it. Try warm soapy water first. If the marks stay, use a non-scratch cream cleaner made for porcelain, testing it first on a spare tile or a hidden corner. Felt pads under chair legs stop the marks coming back. Onyx Cream FAQs: Short Answers

Does Onyx Cream need sealing?

The listing says no. If you are unsure, do the quick grout test on a spare tile described above.

Can I use Onyx Cream on a shower floor?

No. Use it on shower walls, and put a tray or a matt anti-slip tile on the shower floor.

Is Onyx Cream good for a kitchen floor?

Yes, if you wipe spills promptly. Families with young children or pets may prefer a matt cream tile on the floor and Onyx Cream on the walls.

Why is the 60×60cm dearer than the 60×120cm?

The two sizes are priced separately, and on 20 October 2026 the square tile cost £2 more per m². In a small room the difference is only a few pounds, so choose by room size, not price.

How many Onyx Cream tiles are in a square metre?

About 1.39 tiles at 60×120cm or 2.78 tiles at 60×60cm. Either way, a box covers 1.44m².

Can I mix the two sizes in one room?

Yes, for example 60×120cm on the floor and 60×60cm on the walls. Order both at the same time and lay a few side by side first, as shade can vary slightly between production batches.

Is Onyx Cream suitable for outdoor use?

No. It is an indoor tile, and a polished face is unsafe on a wet patio.

Does cream go with gold?

Very well. Brushed brass and gold fittings pick up the caramel tones in the veining.

Who Onyx Cream Suits Best

Onyx Cream suits anyone who wants a warm, light stone look that sits happily with wood, black metal, brass or chrome. It suits busy family homes because cream hides everyday dust better than white. It is also good value at scale: the large size is the cheaper one, and the job lot and pallet bring the price down to between about £23 and £24 per m².

Three choices decide how it turns out. Match the grout to the tile, keep the polish off wet floors, and buy the full amount in one order. Get those right, and a cream floor will look current for years.
