Is Onyx White Real Onyx?

No. Onyx White is porcelain: fired clay with an onyx design on its face and a polish over the top. Natural onyx is a soft, banded stone. It glows when light passes through it, scratches more easily than porcelain and needs regular sealing. The porcelain version keeps the look (milky white clouds and thin grey-white veins) and drops the upkeep. Like the rest of our polished porcelain, the face does not need sealing.

The difference shows when you hold a tile up to the light. Real onyx lets some light through, so it can be backlit. Porcelain is opaque. If you want a backlit bar front or a glowing wall panel, you need real stone. For floors, bathroom walls and a family kitchen, porcelain is the more practical choice. Why does it cost so much less than natural onyx?

Natural onyx is cut from a limited supply of stone, finished by hand and often sold by the slab. Porcelain is pressed and fired in large runs, so the price per square metre is a fraction of the stone price. The lower price does not mean a weaker tile. Porcelain is denser and absorbs far less water than onyx, which is why it holds up on a busy floor.

Should You Choose 60×120cm or 60×60cm?

Price won't decide this, because both sizes cost £23.99 per m². Choose by room size, by how flat your floor is and by who is fitting the tiles.

Choose the Onyx White 60×120cm tile for open-plan floors, long hallways and feature walls. Fewer joints let the veining run further, and on a polished floor that makes the room look more like one continuous surface. The trade-off is handling. Each tile is 1.2m long, it needs a very flat base to avoid lipped edges, and two people should carry it. Our guide to large-format floor tiles by room size covers the room sizes where large tiles pay off.

Choose the Onyx White 60×60cm tile for small bathrooms, cloakrooms and walls with a lot of cut-outs. A square tile is easier to cut around a toilet, a basin pedestal or a window reveal. It also forgives a floor that is flat but not perfect, and it suits a confident DIYer working alone. In a room under about 4m², the large tile often ends up as cut pieces anyway, so you would pay for length you never see.

Where Does Onyx White Work Best, Room by Room?

On a wall, a polished white tile works almost anywhere indoors. On a floor, it depends on how wet the floor gets. Here is how that plays out in each room.

Living rooms and dining rooms: this is where Onyx White looks its best. It is a dry floor, and the gloss bounces daylight deep into the room. A polished white floor shows dust and crumbs sooner than a mid-grey one, so plan on a quick sweep most days.

Hallways: it works, but the first metre inside the front door collects rain and grit from shoes. Put a large barrier mat there. The mat keeps the floor dry where people step in, and it stops grit dulling the polish.

Bathroom walls, including inside the shower: a strong yes. Walls don't need grip, the glaze wipes clean, and the white keeps a small room bright. Browse the full onyx wall tile range if you want a second tone for a niche or a half-height panel.

Kitchen walls and splashbacks: also a yes. Grease and sauce wipe off the polished face with warm soapy water. Our onyx-effect kitchen tiles guide covers splashback layouts in more depth.

Outdoors: no. This is an indoor tile, and a polished face on a patio would be slippery in the rain.

Is Onyx White slippery on a bathroom floor?

When dry, a polished porcelain floor is fine to walk on. When wet, any polished surface gets slippery, and the product listing gives no slip rating for this tile. Our advice is the same as the product page's: you can use Onyx White on a bathroom floor away from the shower and next to the bath, as long as there is a bath mat where people step out. Do not use it as the floor inside a walk-in shower or a level-access wet room.

For the shower floor itself, you have two options. A shower tray, as in the room above, keeps the polished tile out of the water entirely. For a tiled shower floor, use a matt tile with a similar look. The Alba Onyx White matt 60×60cm (£22.99 per m²) is a softer, quieter onyx. Neither listing gives a numbered slip rating either, so if you have a specific grip requirement, such as for a care setting, ask us before you order. Can Onyx White go on a kitchen floor?

Yes, with one condition. A kitchen floor sees spills, so wipe up water and oil when they happen and put a washable runner in front of the sink. If you have young children, older relatives or a dog that skids, a matt tile is the calmer choice for the floor. You can still have Onyx White on the walls.

Does Onyx White work with underfloor heating?

Yes, both sizes are listed as suitable. Porcelain passes heat well, and a polished white floor over heating is a pleasant surprise on a cold morning. Fix it with a flexible cement-based adhesive (S1). The first time you run the system after tiling, bring it up to temperature slowly. How Do You Lay and Look After a Polished White Floor?

Two things decide how good a polished floor looks: a flat base before tiling, and grit-free cleaning afterwards.

Laying: the base must be flat, because any dip shows as a raised edge that catches the light on a glossy surface. Use a levelling clip system with the 60×120cm size. Spread adhesive on the floor and butter the back of each tile so there are no hollow spots. Because the edges are rectified, a 2–3mm joint is enough. Once the adhesive has set, follow our step-by-step grouting guide. Wipe grout haze off the polished face before it hardens.

Cleaning: grit is what dulls a polished tile, not water. Vacuum or sweep with a soft brush, then mop with warm water and a pH-neutral cleaner. Dry-buff with a microfibre cloth if you want a streak-free shine. Skip scouring pads, bleach-heavy sprays and acidic descalers on the grout. Our tile and grout cleaning guide covers stubborn marks. Felt pads under chair and table legs do more to protect the shine than any cleaning product.

Quick Answers About Onyx White

Does Onyx White need sealing?

No. The polished porcelain face does not need sealing. A grout sealer is optional, but it helps keep a white or light grey grout looking clean.

Can I use Onyx White on a shower floor?

Not as the floor of a walk-in shower or wet room. Use a shower tray, or a matt anti-slip onyx tile for the shower floor, and keep Onyx White for the walls.

Does polished porcelain scratch?

Porcelain is hard, but the gloss can lose its sheen where grit is ground in under shoes or chair legs. Barrier mats, felt pads and regular sweeping keep it shiny.

Is the 60×60cm version the same colour as the 60×120cm?

They share a name, a white onyx design and a polished finish. Shade can vary slightly between production batches, so if you mix the sizes in one room, order both together and compare them before fixing.

How many Onyx White tiles do I need for 10m²?

Add 10% for cuts to get 11m², which means 8 boxes (11.52m²). That is 16 tiles at 60×120cm or 32 tiles at 60×60cm.

Can Onyx White be used outside?

No. It is an indoor tile, and a polished face is unsafe on a wet patio.

Is Onyx White good for a small bathroom?

Yes. A white, reflective wall makes a small room feel bigger. The 60×60cm size is easier to fit around a toilet and basin in a tight space. For more white options, browse our onyx-effect bathroom tiles.
