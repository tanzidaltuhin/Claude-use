What does Onyx Diora Gold look like in a real room?

Onyx Diora Gold looks like pale, milky onyx with a few fine gold lines running through it. The base colour sits between ivory and soft white. Wide, cloudy bands of light grey give it the layered, slightly see-through look of real onyx. The gold is a thin amber thread along some of the veins, so it reads as an accent rather than a gold tile.

Light changes how the tile reads. Under warm bulbs of about 2700K, the ivory comes forward and the room feels creamy. Under cool daylight from a north-facing window, the grey bands come forward and the floor looks closer to a light greige. Both are the same tile. This is why our own listings describe it as "ivory with gold" on one size and "grey and gold" on the other.

In search terms, Diora belongs to the white-and-gold and gold onyx family of tiles. It sits at the softest, most ivory end of that family.

The polish is a true high gloss. It mirrors pendant lights and windows, which makes a small room feel brighter and a large room feel deeper. The trade-off is that a gloss face also shows footprints and water marks sooner than a matt one.

How much gold is in Onyx Diora Gold?

Onyx Diora Gold has a small amount of gold, limited to fine amber-gold veins on part of each face. Most of the surface is ivory-white and pale grey. If you want gold that you can see from across the room, look at a tile with heavier gold veining. If you want warmth without the room shouting "gold", Diora is the calmer choice.

Does every Onyx Diora Gold tile look the same?

No two neighbouring tiles look the same, because Onyx Diora Gold is printed with several different faces. The 60×120cm product page alone shows four different faces. Before fixing, lay a few rows out dry and turn or swap tiles until the veins flow without two identical faces sitting side by side.

Is a polished Onyx Diora Gold floor slippery?

A polished Onyx Diora Gold floor is safe to walk on when dry and slippery when wet, like any high-gloss tile. No slip rating is published for this tile, so do not treat it as an anti-slip floor. In the UK, the Health and Safety Executive treats a wet Pendulum Test Value of 36 or more as low slip potential. Polished porcelain usually falls well short of that when wet.

The practical answer is simple. Use it where floors stay dry, wipe spills at once, and put mats where water lands: at the sink, the bath and the back door. For a shower or wet-room floor, use a matt tile from our non-slip bathroom floor tiles range instead. Pale matt tiles sit happily next to Diora walls.

Who should not choose a polished floor?

Homes with elderly relatives, toddlers learning to walk or large dogs are better served by a matt floor. A matt onyx-effect floor keeps a similar look with far less slip risk, and Diora can still go on the walls.

How should Onyx Diora Gold be fitted?

Onyx Diora Gold should be fitted like any large polished porcelain: on a flat, sound surface, with a flexible adhesive, full adhesive cover and a levelling system. A polished face shows every small height difference between tiles under raking light. Good preparation matters more with this tile than with a matt one.

  1. Check the surface. Lay a 2m straightedge across the floor. Fill dips before tiling, because the 60×120cm tile will bridge them and rock.
  2. Use a flexible adhesive. A C2 S1 cement-based adhesive is the usual choice for large porcelain and underfloor heating.
  3. Get full cover. Comb the adhesive with a 10–12mm notched trowel and back-butter each tile, so no hollow spots are left under the gloss.
  4. Keep joints at 2–3mm. Rectified edges allow narrow joints, but do not butt tiles tight together.
  5. Use levelling clips. Put them on every joint of the 60×120cm to stop lippage while the adhesive sets.
  6. Offset by a third, not a half. On the 60×120cm, a brick pattern should move each row by no more than 400mm.
  7. Protect the face. Cover the finished floor with card while other trades work.

Why lay 60×120cm Onyx Diora Gold at a one-third offset?

A one-third offset keeps neighbouring tile edges at closer heights than a half offset. Long porcelain tiles can have a very slight bow along their length. At a half offset, the end of one tile meets the middle of the next, and any bow shows as a ridge. The US standard ANSI A108.02 caps the offset at 33% for tiles with a side over 18 inches (457mm), and many tile makers give the same advice.

Can Onyx Diora Gold go on plasterboard walls?

Onyx Diora Gold can go on sound, primed plasterboard, but it is too heavy for many plaster-skimmed walls. A 9mm porcelain tile weighs roughly 21kg per m², worked out from the usual density of porcelain. The British Standard BS 5385-1 allows about 32kg per m² of tile plus adhesive on plasterboard, and about 20kg per m² on gypsum plaster. In a shower, use cement backer board and a tanking system behind the tiles.

Does the edge type matter for a polished floor?

Rectified edges matter because they let the tiles sit close with thin joints. Thin joints keep the onyx pattern flowing across the floor. Our explainer on rectified porcelain tiles and edge tolerance shows why a rectified tile still needs a small joint.

Questions buyers ask about Onyx Diora Gold

Is Onyx Diora Gold real onyx?

No, Onyx Diora Gold is porcelain printed with an onyx design, not natural onyx stone. Real onyx is a soft, banded stone that scratches, etches and stains easily. Porcelain is far harder and absorbs almost no water: porcelain tiles are defined by water absorption of 0.5% or less under BS EN 14411. The same trade-off applies to marble looks, as our comparison of marble effect porcelain and real marble explains.

Does Onyx Diora Gold need sealing?

The porcelain body does not need sealing, and the grout joints benefit from a grout sealer on light floors. Some polished porcelain has tiny pores opened by the polishing. Test a spare tile before grouting: rub in some grout, wait ten minutes and wipe it off. If a shadow stays, apply a porcelain impregnating sealer to the faces before you grout.

Will a polished porcelain floor scratch or show water spots?

A polished porcelain floor resists everyday wear, but grit dragged under shoes can dull the gloss over time. Doormats at outside doors and felt pads on chair legs prevent most of it. Water spots show on gloss when hard water dries on the surface. Drying the floor with a microfibre mop after washing stops the marks. Unlike marble, porcelain is not usually re-polished at home, so prevention matters more than repair.

How do you clean Onyx Diora Gold?

Clean Onyx Diora Gold with warm water and a pH-neutral floor cleaner, then dry it with a microfibre mop. Avoid wax, oil soaps and abrasive powders, because they leave a film or dull the polish. Our guide to cleaning tiles and grout by tile type covers stubborn marks and grout lines.

Can I use 60×60cm on the floor and 60×120cm on the walls?

Yes, you can mix the two sizes, because both are the same design and finish. The 60×60cm floor is easier to fit around a toilet and basin, while the 60×120cm walls give long, unbroken veins. Order both sizes at the same time to keep the shade as close as possible.

Can Onyx Diora Gold be used outdoors?

No, Onyx Diora Gold is an indoor tile. A wet polished surface is unsafe on a patio, and outdoor tiles are usually thicker and textured. Choose an R11 outdoor porcelain for terraces instead.

Will the colour on my screen match the tile?

Screens show colour differently, so the tile may look warmer or cooler in real life. A cut sample costs £1.99 and a 20×50cm sample costs £7.99. Look at it on the floor in daylight and again in the evening under your own bulbs.
