Seven Words, Decoded: What the Name Tells You

"Statuario" and "Carrara": two marbles in one name

Both are white marbles quarried around Carrara in Tuscany, but they look different. Carrara is the everyday one: a soft grey-white background with fine, feathery grey veins. Statuario is the rarer, whiter stone with bolder, more dramatic veins. Calacatta, the third name people often mix up, is warmer, sometimes with gold tones, and has thicker veins.

This tile borrows from both. It has the bright white ground of Statuario and the soft, flowing grey veining of Carrara, without the heavy dark streaks of a true Statuario slab. The result is calm rather than dramatic, which is why it works across a whole floor and not just one feature wall. To see the whole family side by side, browse our Carrara marble-effect tiles.

"White": how white is it?

It is a clean, slightly cool white. It is not cream, and it is not ivory. Under warm lamps it softens a little, and in north light it looks crisp. Matt surfaces scatter light rather than mirror it, so the white reads as soft and even instead of glaring.

"Matt": the finish

Matt means the glaze has no shine. You see the veining, not reflections of the ceiling lights. The listing describes it as "soft matt with a fine texture that provides grip without feeling rough", which matches how it feels underfoot: smooth to the hand, but not slick.

"Anti-slip": the word that needs the most care

In this product name, "anti-slip" means grippier than polished porcelain. It does not mean "safe on any wet floor". Floor tiles are graded on the R scale, from R9 to R13. The product specification lists this tile as R9, the first step on that scale. That makes it a sound choice for living spaces, hallways, kitchens and bathroom floors outside the shower. It is not a tile for the floor of a walk-in shower or a wet room. More on that in the first myth below.

"Marble effect": what it is made of

The marble look is printed into the glaze of a porcelain tile. There is no stone in it. Porcelain is dense, so it does not etch with lemon juice, stain with red wine or need sealing the way real marble does. The listing confirms that no sealing or polishing is needed. Five Things People Believe About Matt Anti-Slip Tiles, and What's True

Belief 1: "Matt anti-slip means it's safe in the shower."

Not for the shower floor. The R rating comes from a ramp test. A tester in safety boots walks on an oiled tile while the ramp tilts, and the angle at which they slip sets the grade.

At R9, this tile belongs on floors that are usually dry: living rooms, hallways, kitchens and the part of a bathroom away from the shower, with a bath mat where you step out. On the shower floor itself, use a tray, or a tile rated R11. The closest match in our range is the Statuario Mercury Matt Anti-Slip 30×60cm. It has an R11 rating, white-and-grey marble veining and a smaller format that follows the fall to a drain more easily. It is £23.95 per m².

Our non-slip marble bathroom floor guide covers the UK pendulum (PTV) test too, which some specifiers prefer. Inside the shower, this tile works perfectly well on the walls.

Belief 2: "White matt floors always look dirty."

Partly true, and fixable. A matt surface has a fine texture. It holds a thin film of grime longer than a glossy one, and on white that film shows as a greyish cast, especially in kitchens and hallways. Three habits prevent it:

  1. Vacuum or sweep before you mop, so you are not pushing grit around.
  2. Mop with warm water and a pH-neutral tile cleaner, not washing-up liquid. Soap leaves a sticky residue that dirt clings to.
  3. Every few weeks, scrub the walkways with a soft brush and the same cleaner, then rinse with clean water.

Grout matters as much as the tile. Bright white grout in a kitchen will darken, so a pale grey such as Mapei Ultracolor Plus 111 Silver Grey keeps a cleaner look on floors. It also picks up the grey in the veins. For walls, Mapei 100 White keeps the surface seamless. Our tile and grout cleaning guide deals with stains that have already set in.

Belief 3: "Matt tiles soak up water and need sealing."

Not this one. Matt describes the glaze, not the body underneath. Porcelain absorbs very little water, and the listing says no sealing is needed. What can absorb stains is cement grout, so wipe spills off grout lines promptly. A grout sealer is a sensible optional extra in a kitchen.

Belief 4: "Matt looks flat next to polished."

It looks different, not flat. Here is the same design in the same room in both finishes.

The polished floor mirrors the windows and furniture, which looks glamorous but shows every footprint and becomes slippery when wet. The matt floor shows the veining more clearly, because there is no glare on top. It looks closer to honed real marble, the finish stone suppliers use for floors.

If you love the shine for a wall or a dry living room, the polished Statuario Carrara White 60×120cm is the same price, £19.99 per m². Many people use polished on the walls and matt on the floor of the same bathroom.

Belief 5: "Anti-slip tiles feel rough on bare feet."

Some do; this one does not. Heavily textured R11 and R12 tiles can feel coarse. At R9, this tile has a fine texture that you notice more with wet feet than dry ones. The £1.99 cut sample is the quickest way to check: put it on the bathroom floor and stand on it barefoot after a shower.

Fitting Notes From the Trade

A 1.2m tile needs a flat, sound floor, because any hump shows as a raised edge. Use levelling clips, and spread adhesive on both the floor and the back of each tile so there are no hollow spots. Porcelain needs a flexible S1 cement-based adhesive. Because the edges are rectified, a 2mm joint is enough.

Clean grout residue off as you go. Matt surfaces hold onto dried grout haze more than gloss does, and haze is the most common reason a new matt floor looks "dirty" from day one. Our guide to grouting tiles shows how to work in small sections and wipe at the right moment. Lay in a brick bond of no more than one-third offset rather than half. A half offset on long tiles can exaggerate any slight bow in the tile.

Quick-Fire Answers

Is it real marble? No. It is porcelain with a printed marble design, so it needs no sealing and will not etch.

R9 or R10: which is it? The product specification lists R9. Plan on R9: dry and occasionally damp floors, not shower floors.

Can I use it on a wet-room floor? No. Use an R11 tile, such as the Statuario Mercury Matt Anti-Slip 30×60cm, on the wet-room floor, and this tile on the walls.

Is it good for a kitchen floor? Yes, for most homes, as long as spills are wiped up promptly.

Can matt tiles be polished later? No. The finish is fired into the glaze. Choose the polished version from the start if you want shine.

Does it scratch? Porcelain is hard and resists everyday wear. Grit under shoes is the main threat to any finish, so use doormats and felt pads under furniture.

How many tiles per m²? About 1.39. Each box has 2 tiles covering 1.44m².

What goes with white Carrara tiles? Brushed brass or matt black fittings, oak or walnut furniture, and soft grey or sage paint. Avoid a stark blue-white paint, which makes the tile look dull by comparison.

Can I use it outside? No. It is an indoor tile. For patios, choose a 20mm outdoor porcelain.
